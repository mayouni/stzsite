## One program, three languages {#runs}

The idea is the oldest in Softanza: that the thinking can be written as a sentence and the sentence can run. Here is a program as an English paragraph, run as it stands. A word outside the dictionary is not guessed at; the library reads the words it owns and acts on them.

<!--SHOWCASE:natural:1,2,3-->

The Hausa program has different words and the same meaning: every language is a set of words keyed to the same entries of one dictionary, so a sentence in any of them lands on the same operation. The French and Arabic sentences do not translate English word for word, they follow their own grammar, and the Turkish one puts its verb last. Each program says back, in its own language, what it understood.

## Who parses {#parses}

There are two directions, and the difference is who does the reading.

<div class="cards">
<div class="card"><h3>The machine parses</h3><p><code>Naturally()</code> takes a paragraph. The library reads it against its dictionary and must refuse what it cannot prove it understood. Ambiguity is refused loudly. The audience is domain experts and agents.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>The programmer parses</h3><p>A near-natural chain is code that reads as a sentence. The human is the parser, and the chain is the sentence: a chain that runs but does not read as grammatical English is a bug. The audience is programmers.</p></div>
</div>

## What it does not know, it refuses {#refuses}

A system that answers anything is a system you cannot trust with the answer. The natural layer is a closed language: a word it does not know is a literal and never a guess, a misspelled word is named together with the nearest word it does know, and a template with a hole left empty refuses to run.

<!--SHOWCASE:natural:4,5-->

<p class="way"><span>The Softanza way</span> A sentence in your language runs because every word of it is admitted, and the refusal comes with its reason. The naturalness is at the door; the execution happens in a closed language that can be judged.</p>

## Languages are data {#languages}

A language pack is a block of data keyed to the same meanings as English. Five packs ship: English, Hausa in Latin script, French, Arabic and Turkish. Adding one needs no code and no load.

<!--SHOWCASE:natural:6-->

Hausa in Ajami script is named in the library and not shipped; Zarma and Fulfulde are not there yet. They are the next packs to write, and a pack is data.

## Five faces {#faces}

<div class="cards">
<div class="card"><h3>Determinism and sovereignty</h3><p>The natural layer calls no model and needs no key, and the same sentence gives the same answer on any day. That is the condition for a bank or a ministry, not a feature. That no network connection opens during a natural run is not yet measured by a guard of its own.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Accountability</h3><p>Every sentence can say what it understood, what it refused, and why. That is what separates this from code generated by a model and from a toy language for commands.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Languages as data</h3><p>A language is a block, added at runtime. A speaker of Hausa or Arabic writes to the same library as a speaker of English.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Two directions, one discipline</h3><p>The machine that parses and the human who parses share one lexicon: the grammar of the function forms, read as a vocabulary.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>The agent's door <b>(designed)</b></h3><p>A model may draft; a closed grammar judges. Holding a model to the lexicon's grammar, so that it can only produce sentences the layer executes, is designed and not built.</p></div>
</div>

## Where it comes from {#history}

- **2020.** The first slide of the first deck gives the rationale as "expressing the thinking in an executable natural language".
- **The turn.** The author's article on why the natural layer was built states the thesis: a sentence is modelled as code, with no language processing and no machine learning, at native speed, deterministic, with the programmer in control.
- **The three eras.** The layer was built in three stages, each against one wall: the gap between a person's intention and its execution.
- **2026-07-10.** The library's own review found that of fifteen canonical examples run as written, four ran as the articles said. The surface had decayed because no test had ever locked it. It was renovated that day and is held by scenario guards since.

The three articles that tell it are published here with their code run: <a href="narrations/stznatural-narration.html">stzNatural</a>, <a href="narrations/stznatural-vs-ring-naturallib.html">stzNatural against the older natural library</a> and <a href="narrations/stz-near-natural-language-programming.html">near-natural language programming</a>. Several of their blocks no longer run as written, and their pages say which.

## Limits {#limits}

<p class="proof"><b>in construction</b> The vocabulary is bounded by the packs, and each pack covers a part of what the library can do. A program is one object per block. Ambiguity is refused and not resolved. The surface decayed once and is guarded now, which is a promise about the future and not about the past. The article blocks that do not run as written are not yet pinned as examples.</p>
