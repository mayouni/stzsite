The claim is old and it is the first page of the library's writing: functions as linguistic expressions, an embedded language in every name, achieved with no language model, by naming systematically, ordering parameters, varying forms and inferring defaults. The article names the forms. This page does what the site's law asks and tries each one.

<!--GRAMMAR-->

## What was not tried {#not}

Other forms are named in the article and not tried here: the random form, the deep form, the default and free-order forms, the misspelled-name and statement forms, and the free form, whose name collides with another suffix in the code. The multilingual form, a function named in Arabic, French or Chinese, exists only in the library's archive and nowhere live.

<p class="proof"><b>in construction</b> The article documents more than the library has. Seven of the examples above ask for a name the library does not define, and three promise an answer the library does not give: the sections of a plural find, the history of a chain, and what a case-insensitive removal leaves. They are reported to the library, which has the choice between writing the missing forms and correcting the article. The forms that run are the ones the rest of this site uses, and a reader who knows one verb still knows its family: <a href="craft.html">how Softanza is written</a> tells which.</p>
