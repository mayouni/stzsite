Geo & cartography

A map engine that also does the geodesy and the spatial statistics; GIS plumbing is the frontier.

5 Strong 2 Solid 4 Partial 1 Emerging

Measured against: D3-geo · GDAL · PostGIS · Cartopy · Folders: base/geo(opens in a new tab) · For programmers: this area's reference

A map makes claims about the world, so it must say how it was made and assert nothing it cannot check. That sentence is the whole design of Softanza's geo plane, and the guards hold it to it.

Forty-four projections ship with their inverses: closed-form where one exists, otherwise a shared Newton solver that refuses any inverse it cannot round-trip. Geodesy is WGS84 on the ellipsoid, with geodesics, rhumb lines, ECEF and ENU frames, and areas measured on the spheroid. And the same engine that draws the map computes the statistics it shows: Clark-Evans and Ripley's functions with simulation envelopes, kernel densities and contours, inverse-distance weighting, fitted variograms and ordinary kriging with its variance surface. It is the rare mapping tool that also measures its own distortion, with a Tissot indicatrix backed by the same Jacobian as the inverse solver.

o1 = new stzGeoProjection(:mercator)

# what kind of lie this projection tells
? o1.IsConformal()
#--> TRUE

? o1.IsEqualArea()
#--> FALSE

# and how much bigger a place at 70 degrees north is drawn
? o1.ArealScaleAt(0, 70)
#--> 8.55

The map judges its own argument. Colouring regions by value on Mercator is reported as an error, because the colour then speaks for areas the projection has swollen. A scale bar is drawn at a stated latitude and refused on a world Mercator, since no single bar is true across that sheet. Place names are matched without fuzzy guessing: Atlantis is reported back, never coloured, because a map that paints Niger for Nigeria is worse than one with a hole. No border data ships, by decision; a boundary file takes sides and ages silently, so callers bring their own. The real-data map of Niger, official census over open boundaries, is the plane's cap, vendor-free end to end.

Five lanes are Strong. The frontier is GIS plumbing, left on purpose: no overlay algebra (buffer, union, intersection), no reprojection of a dataset's source CRS, no GeoJSON writer, no raster terrain, no web tiles. Live panning belongs to the GUI plane, on this plane's hands.

The one-sentence position. Softanza's geo is analysis-and-cartography strong — a map engine that also does the geodesy and the spatial statistics, coherent in a way D3 and Cartopy are not — and GIS-plumbing light: the overlay algebra, dataset reprojection, raster/terrain, and web tiling that GDAL, PostGIS and Leaflet are built on are the deliberate frontier still to cross.

Rethought from first principles

What Softanza kept from what works, and what it rethought, as the library wrote it down in its design documents and narrations.

The Softanza wayA map makes claims about the world, so it must say how it was made and assert nothing it cannot check.

Kept from the best practice

  • Every map passes through one pipeline borrowed from d3-geo (rotate, cut on the sphere, project, resample, clip), so no picture can skip these steps. source(opens in a new tab)
  • Labels follow four rules shared by QGIS, Mapbox and ArcGIS: a label is a box, big regions speak first, a name that will not fit goes outside, or is dropped. source(opens in a new tab)
  • Colour ramps are Cynthia Brewer's ColorBrewer schemes, written out as data rather than computed, because a computed ramp is one nobody checked. source(opens in a new tab)

Rethought

  • No border data ships with the library. A boundary file takes sides on disputed borders, ages silently and carries a licence, so callers bring or fetch their own. source(opens in a new tab)
  • A map judges its own argument. Colouring regions by value on a projection that swells areas is reported as an error, because the colour then misstates the quantity. source(opens in a new tab)
  • A scale bar is drawn at a stated latitude, and refused when scale varies too much across the sheet, because a bar true along one line misleads everywhere else. source(opens in a new tab)
  • Place names are matched without fuzzy guessing. A near miss is reported, never coloured, because a map that paints Niger for Nigeria is worse than one with a hole. source(opens in a new tab)

12 lanes, each against the best of its category

For every lane: what runs in the engine today, the tools it is fair to measure it against, and what is still owed.

01Map projectionsStrong

In the engine

44 projections (geo_projection.zig) across cylindrical, pseudocylindrical, conic, azimuthal and lenticular families — all with inverses, closed-form where possible else a shared 2D Newton solver with a coarse-search seed that refuses any inverse not round-tripping to 1e-6. Rotate/clip/fit/resample stream transforms and UTM.

Measured against

D3-geo (+ geo-projection)CartopyPROJ

To complete

44 with universal, self-checking inverses exceeds D3-geo's core and Cartopy's built-ins. Below PROJ's hundreds of CRS definitions; Robinson and Albers-USA are documented refusals, and projections are sphere-for-display.

02GeodesyStrong

In the engine

geo_geodesy.zig: WGS84 + an ellipsoid table, ellipsoidal geodesic inverse/direct (Bessel/Karney reduction + quadrature + Vincenty azimuth iteration), rhumb lines, ECEF and ENU frames, DMS parse/format, meridian arc, and polygon area on the ellipsoid with geodesic edges.

Measured against

GeographicLib (Karney)PROJGDAL

To complete

Comprehensive and self-consistent (the Niger map's areas are computed by this very engine). It is a hybrid — not Karney's series end-to-end — but guarded and accurate for the analyst's distances and areas.

03Spatial statisticsStrong

In the engine

geo_stats.zig / geo_interp.zig / geo_process.zig: Clark-Evans with a z-test, Ripley K/L/G/F with Monte-Carlo envelopes, mean centre / spatial median / SD ellipse; IDW, fitted variograms and ordinary kriging with its variance surface (one LU reused over the grid); kernel density with Diggle correction; seven+ point-process null models (Matérn, Thomas, hard-core…).

Measured against

spatstat (R)PySALArcGIS

To complete

Broadly at spatstat-core parity — the rare lane where a mapping library also computes the statistics it draws. Kriging variance depends on geometry, not values, and is guarded to prove it.

04Thematic mapsStrong

In the engine

Choropleth (equal-area default, data-driven standard parallel, north-up guard), proportional symbol (area-encoded), flow (great circles), hex-bin (engine-backed), bloc/membership, and insets with a measured locator scale — all with honesty rules and refusals.

Measured against

D3GeoPandas plotkepler.gl

To complete

A broad thematic set for the maps an analyst actually makes. The two missing kinds are dot-density and cartogram.

05Map decorations & fieldsStrong

In the engine

geo_furniture.zig: graticule, Tissot indicatrix (backed by the same Jacobian as the inverse solver), a scale bar that refuses to lie, a measured north arrow, a full day-night terminator + twilight + night cap, and vector fields with even-spaced streamlines.

Measured against

CartopyD3matplotlib / basemap

To complete

A richer, more measured decoration set than most — the terminator, streamlines and self-consistent Tissot are beyond the usual furniture. Nothing structural owed here.

06GeoJSON / TopoJSON I/OSolid

In the engine

stzGeoFeatures reads both GeoJSON and TopoJSON, keeping every part, hole, property and id, with an engine-side filtered read so a 40 MB admin file yields only the wanted features.

Measured against

GDAL / OGRGeoPandasD3

To complete

Read-only — there is no GeoJSON/TopoJSON writer, so geometry can come in but not out (output is PNG/SVG pictures). And only two formats, next to GDAL's dozens.

07Atlas & real-data pipelineSolid

In the engine

stzGeoAtlas binds name→shape without vendoring boundary data, reporting the unresolved and uncovered. The Niger real-data map is the cap: geoBoundaries ADM1 (ODbL) + official RGPH-2012 census, with areas measured by the house geodesic engine, rendered as a density choropleth.

Measured against

GeoPandas + Natural EarthCartopy features

To complete

A real, vendor-free end-to-end map is a genuine capability — but the atlas ships no bundled shapes (by design) and has no fuzzy name matching, so a caller must supply and exactly name its boundaries.

08Geometry & spatial opsPartial

In the engine

Measurement and membership: ring area (planar/spherical/ellipsoidal), centroid, point-in-polygon / Within, bounding boxes, and spatial join (count/assign points to regions, coverage fraction).

Measured against

PostGIS / GEOSShapelyGDAL

To complete

No vector-geometry overlay algebra: no buffer, intersection, union, difference, convex hull, simplify or dissolve. The PostGIS core is a deliberate non-goal today.

09Source reprojection (CRS transforms)Partial

In the engine

Projections are a display transform — lon/lat in, pixels out, with all the forward/inverse math above.

Measured against

PROJGDAL warpCartopy CRS

To complete

No source-CRS reprojection: a dataset stored in UTM or a national grid can't be transformed to another CRS — features keep their stored CRS and lon/lat input is assumed. The projection math exists; the CRS-transform plumbing does not.

10Raster & DEMPartial

In the engine

geo_field.zig: scalar density fields, an ESRI ASCII grid reader, a field image resampled per-pixel through the projection, and contours by marching squares with polygon clipping.

Measured against

GDAL (raster)rasterioCartopy imshow

To complete

No DEM terrain analysis (hillshade, slope, aspect) and no general raster formats (GeoTIFF). Scalar fields + ESRI-grid are the whole raster story.

11InteractivityPartial

In the engine

Maps emit interactive SVG — per-region ids, classes and &lt;title&gt; hooks (SetInteractive, GE2c) — so a browser can hover and select.

Measured against

LeafletMapbox GLD3 zoom

To complete

No live pan/zoom — interaction stops at static SVG hooks. Live map interaction is, by the plan, the GUI plane's job on the geo plane's hands, not the geo module's own.

12Tiling & web mapsEmerging

In the engine

None. There is no slippy-tile scheme, no tile server, and no web-map basemap integration anywhere in the geo module.

Measured against

Leaflet / MapboxXYZ tile serversOpenLayers

To complete

The whole web-map delivery model — tiled basemaps, zoom levels, tile serving. Softanza renders a finished map, not a pannable tiled surface.

What is owed, in the order of return

The gaps, ordered by what they bring and not by their size; value is the effect on the library's reach, effort an engineer's reading.

Tier A — GIS-data workflows

Vector geometry overlay algebra

Buffer, intersection, union, difference, convex hull, simplify, dissolve — the PostGIS/GEOS core. It is the biggest capability gap for real GIS work and unblocks everything from catchment analysis to boundary cleaning.

Value · highEffort · high
Source-CRS reprojection

Transform a dataset from its stored CRS (UTM, national grid) to another — the PROJ/GDAL-warp core. The forward/inverse projection math already ships; this is the CRS-graph and datum-shift plumbing on top of it.

Value · highEffort · medium

Tier B — cheap breadth over a strong base

A GeoJSON / TopoJSON writer

Close the round-trip — geometry can be read but not written today. A writer lets geo output feed other tools and lets the atlas persist resolved shapes. Small next to the reader that ships.

Value · mediumEffort · low
Dot-density & cartogram

The two thematic types not yet in the set. Both sit naturally on the existing region/point machinery and complete the analyst's thematic toolkit.

Value · mediumEffort · medium
Close GE0c — fill across a seam

The plan's one open item: reconstruct a closed fill for a polygon cut by the antimeridian or horizon (today such pieces are stroked, not filled). A finishing touch on an otherwise complete projection renderer.

Value · lowEffort · medium

Tier C — delivery & terrain

Raster / DEM & GeoTIFF

Terrain analysis (hillshade, slope, aspect) over elevation, and general raster I/O (GeoTIFF). The scalar-field and contour engine is the seed; DEM ops and real raster formats are the reach.

Value · mediumEffort · high
Tiling & web maps

Slippy tiles, zoom levels and basemap integration — the Leaflet/Mapbox delivery model. It turns a finished map into a pannable surface for the web.

Value · mediumEffort · high
Live interactivity via the GUI plane

Pan / zoom / brush on the interactive-SVG hands the geo plane already exposes (GE2c). Belongs on the GUI plane's reactive layer, and would make the strong static maps explorable.

Value · mediumEffort · high
