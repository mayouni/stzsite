stzMathFigure

34 own methods · 3 forms written with extensions, listed under their method

the source: base/math/stzMathFigure.ring(opens in a new tab) · All classes · The area reference: Numeric & optimization

A mathematical figure declared by what it is, a function, a residual plot, a field, and solved into a picture whose every mark says what it stands for.

aDeaths = [
    [ 14, 15, 14 ],
    [  7,  4,  7 ],
    [  8,  2, 10 ],
    [ 15,  9, 10 ],
    [  0,  2,  0 ]
]

aNames = [
    [ "1-24", "25-74", "75-199", "200++", "NA" ],
    [ "1973", "1974", "1975" ]
]

oRes = StzMathFigureQ(:ResidualPlot, [
    :of = aDeaths,
    :names = aNames
])

# the figure says what it shows
? StzFindFirst("residual-versus-fit", oRes.Why()) > 0
#--> TRUE

# and no rule found a defect in it
? len( oRes.Violations() )
#--> 0
run in the library on 2026-10-21

Part of it is computed, from the domain's own formulas, and part is solved, the layout of the marks so that nothing overlaps and every note finds its place; the figure says what was computed and what was solved in one sentence each. Rules judge the layout, so a defect is a violation that can be counted and not an image that looks a little off. The picture can be themed without being solved again, and a mark can be pinned or dragged while the rest settles around it.

It is the picture of a median polish, of a function with its zeros and extrema marked, of a figure in a lesson that must be exact. It serves the analyst who wants the residuals seen and the teacher who wants the curve right.
