stzDate

92 own methods · 26 forms written with extensions, listed under their method

the source: base/datetime/stzDate.ring(opens in a new tab) · All classes · The area reference: Internationalization

A day of the calendar, without its hours: it reads a date in the form that arrives, does the arithmetic of days, weeks, months and years on it, and knows the calendar, leap years and the length of a month included.

It is written back for whichever reader is asking, in the European order, the American order, the ISO form, or any shape you name.

o1 = StzDateQ("15/07/2024")

# the same day, written for three readers
? o1.ToEuropean()
#--> 15/07/2024

? o1.ToAmerican()
#--> 07/15/2024

? o1.ToISO8601()
#--> 2024-07-15

# and in any shape you name
? o1.ToStringXT("dddd, MMMM d, yyyy")
#--> Monday, July 15, 2024
run in the library on 2026-10-20

Arithmetic follows the calendar, not the addition of a number: twenty days after the fifteenth of March is the fourth of April, and a question about the calendar is answered by the date itself.

o1 = StzDateQ("15/03/2024")

# twenty days later crosses into April
o1.AddDays(20)
? o1.Date()
#--> 04/04/2024

o2 = StzDateQ("15/02/2024")

# the calendar is known, leap years included
? o2.IsLeapYear()
#--> TRUE

? o2.DaysInMonth()
#--> 29
run in the library on 2026-10-20

A date can be compared with another, measured against it in days, asked what week of the year it falls in, converted to a Julian day. It is the day that stzDateTime splits off, and the day that stzCalendar and stzTimeLine are laid out in.
