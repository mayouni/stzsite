stzText

82 own methods · 28 forms written with extensions, listed under their method · Also inherits from stzStringText (113)

the source: base/linguistic/stzText.ring(opens in a new tab) · All classes · The area reference: NLP & linguistics

stzText is a string raised to meaning: the same characters, now read as words, sentences, names, tone and topics, with nothing to install or download.

Build it from any text, new stzText("...") or Q("...").TextQ(), and ask in plain words. Sentiment() says positive, negative or neutral, and IsPositive() answers with its confidence, certainly, probably or apparently, and a Why(). NamedEntities() finds people, organisations and places; POSTags() labels every word; NounPhrases() applies the classic noun-phrase shape; Stemmed() and Lemmatized() reduce words to their root or dictionary form, in 25 languages for stems and three for lemmas; KeyPhrases(), RankedKeywords() and Summary() extract what matters; ReadingEase() grades the prose.

Every one of these is a published reference algorithm embedded in the engine, Snowball, VADER, NLTK's tagger, TextRank, WordNet, so a first call works offline the moment the library loads. When a local model is present, the same text also answers Embedding(), SemanticSimilarityWith(), ClassifiedAs(labels) and SummarizedAbstractively(); without one they fall back or stay empty, honestly, and HasSemanticModel() says which case you are in.

It serves wherever text must be understood rather than merely stored: reading feedback, sorting messages, finding who and where a document speaks of, sizing the reading level of a lesson, building the vocabulary of a world from the texts that describe it.

IsPositive

A verdict with its confidence: emphatic praise is certainly positive, a flat statement only apparently not.

# an emphatic praise is CERTAINLY positive
? Q("I absolutely love this wonderful amazing library!").TextQ().IsPositive()
#--> 1

# ...
? Evidentially()
#--> certainly

# ...
? Why()
#--> certainly yes: positive in tone

# a neutral statement is only APPARENTLY not positive
? Q("The sky is blue.").TextQ().IsPositive()
#--> 0

# ...
? Evidentially()
#--> apparently

# ...
? Why()
#--> apparently not: positive in tone

IsSemanticallySimilarTo

A close paraphrase is probably similar, an unrelated text certainly not, and Why() says so in words.

oT = Q("cats are wonderful pets").TextQ()
# a close paraphrase is PROBABLY similar
? oT.IsSemanticallySimilarTo("dogs are wonderful pets", 0.5)
#--> 1

# ...
? Evidentially()
#--> probably

# ...
? Why()
#--> probably yes: semantically similar to "dogs are wonderful pets"

# an unrelated text is CERTAINLY not
? Q("the stock market crashed").TextQ().IsSemanticallySimilarTo("kittens love to play", 0.5)
#--> 0

# ...
? Why()
#--> certainly not: semantically similar to "kittens love to play"

ClassifiedAs

A text sorted under the nearest of the labels you give.

t1 = new stzText("the stock market prices fell sharply on wall street today")
# ClassifiedAs picks the closest label
? t1.ClassifiedAs([ "market finance", "cooking food" ])
#--> market finance

# Classify returns ranked [label, score] pairs, best first
? t1.Classify([ "market finance", "cooking food" ])[1][1]
#--> market finance

# Classify keeps all labels
? len(t1.Classify([ "market finance", "cooking food", "space travel" ]))
#--> 3

# ClassifyQ is the basic stzList
? classname(t1.ClassifyQ([ "a", "b" ]))
#--> stzlist

# ClassifyQQ is stzListOfPairs
? classname(t1.ClassifyQQ([ "a", "b" ]))
#--> stzlistofpairs

# a non-list of labels is rejected
? @@(t1.Classify("not a list"))
#--> [ ]

Chunks
POS-PATTERN CHUNKING (R3, the NLTK offensive): PATTERNS OVER TAGS. ? Q("The quick brown fox...").TextQ().Chunks("DT? JJ* NN+") #--> [ "The quick brown fox", "the lazy dog" ] Grammar: Penn tags separated by spaces, each with an optional quantifier -- ? (0/1), * (0+), + (1+), none (exactly 1). A tag matches by PREFIX (NN covers NN/NNS/NNP/NNPS). Covers NLTK's RegexpParser chunking with a cleaner grammar. The executor is a deterministic greedy scanner over the tag stream (like NLTK's); a Listex-native executor can swap in behind this same surface.no extension
