Search
The distance is SQUARED Euclidean over the stored vectors -- squared because the square root changes nothing about the ordering and costs a call per candidate. In :Cosine mode the vectors are unit-length, so the distance is 2 - 2*cos and CosineSimilarityTo() reads it back as a cosine.
Nearest
Same as Search: The distance is SQUARED Euclidean over the stored vectors -- squared because the square root changes nothing about the ordering and costs a call per candidate. In :Cosine mode the vectors are unit-length, so the distance is 2 - 2*cos and CosineSimilarityTo() reads it back as a cosine.
SearchWithBudget
The same, with the candidate budget stated. Bigger budget, better recall, more work. 0 asks for the default.
SearchExact
The exact answer by full scan -- O(n) and the ground truth. Use it on a small corpus, or to measure what the approximate path is missing.
NearestExact
Same as SearchExact: The exact answer by full scan -- O(n) and the ground truth. Use it on a small corpus, or to measure what the approximate path is missing.
RecallAgainstExact
THIS IS THE METHOD THAT MAKES THE INDEX HONEST. Measure it on your own vectors before trusting a budget -- recall depends on the data, not just on the settings, and no default can promise a number.
CosineFromDistance
Cosine similarity of a stored vector to a query, recovered from the stored distance. Only meaningful in :Cosine mode, where both are unit vectors and d^2 = 2 - 2*cos.
