The journeys

A library is usually presented by its anatomy. Softanza is also presented by who you are and what you are doing: one journey per person, the steps in the order they take them, and under each step the part of Softanza that serves it, its stage of construction and a page that shows it.

The seven doors of the Audiences page say what each reader declares and gets; the journeys say how they get there.

Every step carries its stage, in the words of the Estate page: built, in construction, specification, designed, named. A journey may be published with steps that are not built; it says so at every one.

Nine journeys

The programmer

You write the application you were asked for, and Softanza is the part of it that reads like a sentence, is correct in every script, and scales from one value to many cores.

5 steps: 4 built, 1 in construction

The educator

You turn what you know into units a learner can run, and you can say what a learner has proved, with the limits of that claim beside it.

4 steps: 2 built, 1 designed, 1 named

The linguist and the knowledge architect

You declare the domain and its information architecture, and the library gives you a deterministic model of it, with no neurons, that answers from the graph and says why.

5 steps: 4 built, 1 named

The analyst and the designer

You write the specification as sentences, and what you hand over is a prototype that runs and a deployment that has been rehearsed.

4 steps: 3 built, 1 in construction

The data analyst

You bring data in, clean it with the verbs you already read, ask it questions in sentences, and end on a chart you can show.

5 steps: 4 built, 1 specification

The technical leader

You declare your world, your governance and what each actor may commit, and you get a platform whose every promise has a judge. You buy from an architect: the platform architect's journey is the one that builds it.

5 steps: 3 built, 1 in construction, 1 named

The agent

You declare the agent in a file, you give it a world to stand on, and a rule you can read says what it may commit, which for a model is nothing.

5 steps: 4 built, 1 designed

The author

You write an article whose code is real, and you publish it with the date it was run, in the languages your readers read.

4 steps: 3 built, 1 in construction

The platform architect

You declare a platform floor by floor, from the machine up to the organisation, and every floor is a text file you own with a judge beside it. This is the journey that crosses the whole vertical.

9 steps: 7 built, 1 specification, 1 designed

Focus without restriction

Softanza serves any algorithmic need, and the Atlas is the proof of breadth. It is best made for the domains where its lanes are strong, and that focus is a measurement that moves with the library: the journeys stand on those domains and the Atlas keeps the rest reachable.
