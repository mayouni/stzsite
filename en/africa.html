Born in Africa

Softanza is designed by a Tunisian, between Tunisia, Niamey and Paris. Its references are a bank, a customs school and a restaurant. Its course speaks Hausa. Its machine is named after a bridge over the Niger River.

Three cities

Tunisia, where the author lives and where Organizium was born in work with a bank and a school. Niamey, where the vision was tested with students, a bank under BCEAO supervision, and a panel on governing AI. Paris and Lyon, where a couscous restaurant became the first customer of a platform for neighbourhood commerce.

The references, with their document

Sonibank, Niamey

Organizium Standard Edition, under licence, installed on the bank's internal network. The delivered installation guide names the licence file and the prerequisites; that document is what attests the reference.

Source: the installation guide of the Sonibank deliverable (delivery repository, private).

The National Customs School, Tunisia

When Organizium was proposed to it, four requirements surfaced and turned the assessment architecture from a survey tool into an organisational platform. The four requirements are documented one by one.

Source: Zin's assessment architecture, section "customer-validated architecture" (private repository).

RestoLean, Lyon

A platform for neighbourhood commerce, carried by the owner of a couscous restaurant in Lyon and designed by the author. Amendment no. 1 closed on 4 August 2026; the ergonomic rule is "at most two gestures per action".

Source: the RestoLean mission file (delivery repository, private).

Nothing else is a reference until a document proves it. This site quotes no number of countries and no number of developers.

The course speaks Hausa

The Elementary Introduction and the mathematics course exist in English, French, Arabic and Hausa: thirty chapters in Hausa, every cell of which runs. The natural layer understands an instruction in Hausa and runs it: tonight, "Yi jeri dauke [ 5, 3, 5, 1 ] cire maimaitattu" answered [ 5, 3, 1 ]. The Hausa edition awaits review by a native speaker, and Zarma does not exist yet: both are invitations. See the Learn page.

Harobanda: the name of a bridge

The family's declared machine is called Harobanda. It is named for the bridge across the Niger River that joins the two banks of Niamey, "because the machine likewise joins a solution's promises to the hardware that keeps them". It had another name until 20 September 2026; it has been public, under the MIT licence, since the 27th.

github.com/mayouni/harobanda, section "A note on names".

A simulator for Niamey

The "Tomate Niamey" simulator is an interactive page born of a conversation with students: a market, prices, decisions, and what they cost. Open the simulator.

The Niamey panel on governing AI (April 2026, "govern AI before it governs us") and the DIKO proposal in French and local languages are cited in the vision corpus, chapter 08, section 1.3; that corpus is not public yet.
